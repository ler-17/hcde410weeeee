**Research Question** 
How does the use of swearing and other taboo langauge (ex. sensitive topics) vary with respect to gender expression in spoken word poetry?

**Dataset** 
Spoken word poetry uploaded to instagram from popular poetry accounts along with the user's self-reported gender expression, anonymous coding of poetry at two live poetry events (one predominately white, one predominately black), anonymous interviews of local spoken word poets. 


**Motivation & problem** 
Penny Eckert and other 3rd wave sociolinguists have discussed the impact of gender expression with language variation. How individuals construct their gender identity determines and is determiend by the meaning they assign, consciously or not, to langauge use. Construction and expression of identity, including gender identity, is a prominent theme in spoken word poetry groups, settings that also show a wide variety of langauge variation. However, as poetry is often underrepresented in linguistic research, their interrelationship has not yet been researched. Preliminary investigation of the interrelationship between gender expression and langauge variation, specifically use of swear words and discussion of taboo topics (eg. sexual violence, political autonomy, etc) is the goal of the present study. 

**Data** 
This study will be a mixed-methods investigation combining the qualitative data from interviews with the quantitative coding of poems and self-reported gender information.
